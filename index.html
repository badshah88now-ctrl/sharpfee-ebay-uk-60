eBay Fees in the UK: What a £60 Order Really Costs a Business Seller

Selling a £60 item on eBay in the UK as a business seller is not free, and the final value fee is the part most new sellers underestimate. eBay charges a percentage of the total sale plus a small per-order fee, and on a £60 order that adds up fast.

Business sellers pay a final value fee that varies by category. In Crafts, Pet Supplies, Event Tickets and the Everything Else category it is 12.9% — that is £7.74 on a £60 order. On top of that, eBay charges a per-order fee of £0.40 for orders over £10, plus a regulatory operating fee of 0.35%, another £0.21. Add them together and the fees on a £60 UK business order come to about £8.35, leaving you with £51.65 before postage and materials.

Private sellers get a very different deal: UK-based private sellers pay 0% final value fees on most categories. The catch is that private selling is not a business — once you are selling regularly for profit, eBay expects a business account, and the 12.9% applies.

That is nearly 14% of the sale price gone before you have paid for anything else. Compare that with running your own Shopify store. Shopify UK Basic costs £25 a month, and each order costs 2% plus 25p in card fees — about £1.45 on a £60 order. At that rate, your own store becomes cheaper than eBay once you pass roughly four £60 orders a month. Below that volume, eBay's built-in traffic is worth the higher per-order fee. Above it, you are paying eBay for customers you could increasingly find yourself.

The practical move for a UK seller is to price with the full £8.35 in mind, not just the headline percentage.
